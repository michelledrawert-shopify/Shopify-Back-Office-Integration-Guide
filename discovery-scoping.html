Discovery & Scoping — ERP-Involved Shopify Builds

The working toolkit for discovering and scoping a Shopify project that involves an ERP — D2C or B2B. Includes copyable templates you can drop straight into your discovery doc or SOW. Pairs with the ERP Launch Playbook.

Unofficial field reference — not official Shopify documentation. Always verify against the Shopify Help Center, Shopify.dev, and the ERP/connector vendor's docs. Spot something off? Open an issue.  ·  Last updated: June 2026

Why this discovery is different

A standard Shopify discovery is mostly about the storefront — brand, merchandising, theme, marketing. An ERP-involved discovery also has to scope a second system and the contract between the two. Three things change:

Different people in the room

Not just ecom/marketing — you need IT, the ERP admin/owner, finance/ops, the ERP's implementer or VAR, and (for B2B) the wholesale lead. Discovering with only the ecom stakeholder is how these projects drift.

A decision a normal discovery skips

The system-of-record map: for every data domain, which system owns it after launch and which way it syncs. Leave it implicit and the build thrashes. (Template below.)

Sequencing & data readiness

Is an ERP migration also underway, and how clean is the ERP's data? These are scope-defining questions that don't exist on a pure storefront project.

Who to get in the room

  • Merchant ecom / digital lead — storefront, merchandising, go-live goals.
  • IT / ERP admin or owner — the ERP's reality: version, hosting, APIs, existing integrations.
  • Finance / operations — financials, tax, reconciliation expectations.
  • ERP implementer / VAR — who actually builds/maintains the ERP side (often a separate firm).
  • Wholesale / sales lead (B2B) — contract pricing, buyer structure, how reps order today.
  • The integration owner — one accountable person on each side (partner + merchant).

From discovery to scope

Split into workstreams

Storefront build · integration · data migration · ERP-side changes. Each can have its own track and owner.

Turn the SoR map into a contract

Convert the map into a concrete list: object × direction × frequency × phase.

Define minimum-viable integration

The "Y to launch" rows = Phase 0. Everything else is a later phase. Protect the launch date.

Sequence concurrent work

If an ERP migration is also happening, decouple timelines — usually launch Shopify on the current ERP first.

Signal the complexity

Low / Med / High from the drivers: no certified connector, on-prem/legacy, real-time bidirectional, customization, B2B primitives.

Name owners & log risks

One integration owner per side; capture dependencies, assumptions, and the risks you'd flag to the merchant.

Put external dependencies in the plan

List every party outside your team on the critical path — ERP VAR, merchant IT, connector/tax vendor — each with an owner and a lead time, and review them in every status update. Their work won't move just because yours is ready; "waiting on the VAR" is the usual reason these timelines slip.

How to use this: run the discovery with the right people in the room, fill the system-of-record map and question set live, then translate them into the scope skeleton. The map is the single most valuable artifact — it turns "integrate the ERP" from a vague line item into a concrete, phaseable contract. Pair it with a named list of external dependencies and lead times, reviewed every status update — that's the part that protects the date.
