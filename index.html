ERP Launch Playbook Discovery & Scoping Integration Reference

Launching Shopify with an ERP — A Partner Playbook

A field guide for agency partners bringing a merchant onto Shopify when an ERP is in the picture. The ERP integration is the part of these projects most often underestimated — here's how to scope, sequence, and phase it so the storefront launches on time and the integration lands cleanly. Built to be useful whether it's your first ERP build or your hundredth.

Unofficial field reference — not official Shopify documentation. Always verify against the Shopify Help Center, Shopify.dev, and the ERP/connector vendor's docs before scoping. Spot something off or out of date? Open an issue.  ·  Last updated: June 2026

What a smooth ERP-involved launch looks like

Two systems, clear roles

Shopify is the storefront & order layer; the ERP stays the system of record for pricing, inventory & finance. You're connecting two systems — not making one replace the other.

Integration is its own workstream

Scope it, staff it, and give it its own timeline — planned in discovery, separate from the storefront build. It rarely fits as a single line item.

Launch on minimum-viable integration

Define the smallest sync needed to go live (often just orders → ERP) and phase the rest after launch. The storefront doesn't wait for full ERP parity.

One integration owner

Name a single accountable owner for the ERP integration. When several partners share a project with no clear owner, it drifts.

Pick your play

1

Integrating an existing ERP

The situation: the merchant already runs an ERP (NetSuite, Business Central, Acumatica, SAP, etc.) and wants it connected to their new Shopify store.

What good looks like
  1. Identify the integration path first. Certified connector, middleware (iPaaS), app, or custom — see the Integration Reference. This one fact sets the budget and the timeline.
  2. Agree the minimum sync to launch (often orders → ERP), and phase the rest: inventory, products, customers, fulfillment, financials.
  3. Launch the storefront on that minimum, then expand the integration in planned phases after go-live.
Scope these in discovery
  • Which ERP and version; cloud or on-prem; which objects must sync and in which direction; real-time vs batch; who owns/builds the connector; the tax engine.
Complexity drivers — budget more time (and likely a specialist) when you see these
  • No certified connector for that ERP · on-prem or legacy ERP · bidirectional real-time sync required · a heavily-customized ERP instance.
2

An ERP migration and a Shopify build at the same time

The situation: the merchant is replacing or upgrading their ERP and moving to Shopify concurrently.

What good looks like
  1. Decouple the timelines. Don't run both critical paths in lockstep — that's what stretches the Shopify launch.
  2. Sequence deliberately. The cleanest path is usually: launch Shopify against the current ERP first, then migrate the ERP afterward. If the new ERP genuinely must come first, build a thin interim integration so the storefront can still launch on schedule.
  3. Define the minimum-viable integration for go-live; everything else is post-launch.
Set this expectation explicitly with the merchant: the Shopify go-live is not gated on the ERP project finishing. Two big programs colliding is the classic reason a storefront slips for months.
3

B2B where the ERP is the source of truth

The situation: a wholesale/B2B build where the ERP owns the customer accounts, contract pricing, and the order record. This is the one partners most often struggle to scope — so here's the framework.

What good looks like — decide where each piece lives
  • Catalog & products: synced from the ERP, or maintained in Shopify.
  • Pricing: per-company contract pricing from the ERP → native B2B catalogs, or live pricing via Cart Transform when pricing is too dynamic or too large for catalogs.
  • Companies, locations & buyers: native B2B primitives — confirm your connector/middleware is B2B-aware (the most common wrong assumption).
  • Order flow: orders placed in Shopify flow to the ERP; define payment terms and draft/PO approval.
  • Payment: terms vs. card; make sure orders close out in Shopify.
The goal is to make Shopify the trusted transacting layer — wholesale orders flowing through Shopify to the ERP, not Shopify configured while orders still get placed in the ERP.
Phase it to build momentum
  • Prove the ERP ↔ Shopify B2B loop on one segment, region, or catalog first, then expand. A full big-bang B2B scope is what feels "too big to start" — phasing is how you actually get live.

Scope it in discovery — the questions that set you up to win

  • System of record: which system owns products, inventory, pricing, customers, and the order record — and where does each need to live in the launched state?
  • ERP details: which ERP, which version, cloud or on-prem, and is it customized?
  • Integration path: is there a certified connector, a middleware option, an app, or is this custom? (Start in the Integration Reference.)
  • Objects & direction: which objects sync (orders, products, inventory, customers, fulfillment, financials) and which way — and which are needed to launch vs. later?
  • Timing: real-time or batch? What's the minimum-viable integration to go live?
  • Concurrent projects: is an ERP migration also underway? If so, what's the sequence and what's the interim integration?
  • B2B specifics: contract pricing model, company/buyer structure, payment terms, approval flows — and is the connector B2B-aware?
  • Ownership: who is the single accountable owner of the integration, on the partner side and the merchant side?

Full version — a copyable discovery question set, a system-of-record map, and a scope skeleton — in the Discovery & Scoping toolkit.

Design within these from day one

Reconcile on Shopify's IDs. Match records on order.id and reconcile payments on payment_id (with Shopify Payments) — not on the order name or a gateway-specific ID.

B2B pricing at scale. Native catalogs are reliable into the low tens of thousands; for very large or highly dynamic contract pricing, use live pricing via Cart Transform.

Large orders. Cart/checkout/orders cap at 500 line items — plan for distributors/wholesale with very large baskets.

POS path. The ERP integrates to Shopify admin, not directly to POS. The Purchase Order API isn't GA yet (only Transfers) — verify before scoping POS receiving.

Confirm B2B coverage. Don't assume a connector supports B2B primitives (companies, catalogs, price lists) — verify it for the specific tool before you promise B2B.
